# CUUED

**Queue up. Duel a friend.** A one-on-one arena shooter that runs in the browser at **[cuued.com](https://cuued.com)**. Shoot, block and wall-jump to win points. The loser of each round drafts one card from a hand of five, so every loss lets you build a counter to your rival.

The name is said like *queued*: pick a name, share a 4-letter code, and you're in a duel. No download, no account.


## Play

Go to [cuued.com](https://cuued.com), or open `index.html` in a browser. There's no build step, server or dependencies to install. A copy also runs on [GitHub Pages](https://justincart23.github.io/counterpick/).

The first time you open it, you pick a name. It's remembered in your browser, and you can change it from the title screen or Settings.

### Online play

1. One player picks **Play online → Host a game** and gets a 4-letter room code and an invite link.
2. The other player opens the link, or picks **Play online → Join with a code** and types the code.
3. The match starts as soon as they connect. Both players draft at the start, and each round's loser drafts on their own screen.

Online play is peer-to-peer. The host's browser runs the match, and the guest sends its inputs and draws the host's snapshots. The guest's own fighter is **predicted locally**, so it responds instantly, and it's corrected against the host's acknowledged state without visible jumps. A correction never nudges a standing fighter down into the ledge it stands on, and if the guest's screen ever lets it slip under a wooden ledge the host still has it standing on, it's put straight back on top (before this, a cross-country guest could bob under a ledge, drawn half inside the stone below). While a correction glides in, the drawn body catches up four times as fast if it would be drawn sunk into a floor or wall. The free public [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com) broker only introduces the two browsers, so there is no game server to run.

When two networks can't link directly (strict routers, campus or office Wi-Fi, some phone carriers), the game **falls back to a relay automatically**. Messages go through free public MQTT brokers ([EMQX](https://www.emqx.com/en/mqtt/public-mqtt5-broker), then [Mosquitto](https://test.mosquitto.org)) over secure WebSockets, with no account needed. The host listens on every broker, and the guest uses the first one that answers. A relayed match adds about 40–80 ms each way, shows `ONLINE (RELAY)` at the top of the screen, and sends snapshots at 20 Hz instead of 30. These are free test brokers with no uptime guarantee, which suits a game played with friends. `node scripts/relay-smoke.js` checks them live.

**Optional upgrade: Cloudflare TURN.** `api/turn.js` is a Vercel function that can hand out short-lived [Cloudflare Realtime TURN](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/) credentials (free for the first 1 TB a month). That keeps even blocked networks on a fast WebRTC path, so the MQTT fallback is rarely needed. To enable it, set two environment variables on the Vercel project and redeploy:

| Variable | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| `CF_TURN_KEY_ID` | Cloudflare dashboard > Realtime > TURN Server > your key's **Turn Token ID** |
| `CF_TURN_KEY_TOKEN` | The **API Token** shown once when you create that key |

Without them, `/api/turn` returns STUN only and the MQTT relay covers blocked networks. `node scripts/turn-check.js` confirms the credentials work by allocating (and releasing) a real relay slot.

| | Move | Jump | Drop through a plank | Aim | Shoot | Block |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **You** | `A` `D` or `←` `→` | `W` / `Space` / `↑` | `S` / `↓` | mouse | click | right-click / `Shift` |

Press `Esc` to pause and `M` to mute. In the menus, use `↑` `↓` to choose, `Enter` to select, `←` `→` to change a setting and `Esc` to go back.

## How a match works

1. Both players draft one card before the first shot.
2. A point ends when one fighter dies. Two points win a round.
3. The round's loser drafts one card from a hand of five. Cards stack.

**The draft (every versus mode):** everyone who picks gets their own hand of five and picks at the same time, CPUs included. You only ever see your own hand. Each picker gets a face-down card that ticks when they lock in. Once everyone has, the cards flip face up one by one, so you see what the others took but never what they passed on. Online, each screen is sent only its own player's hand. Players sharing one screen pick one after another before the flip. With anyone on another screen there is a 25 s limit, and a card is picked for anyone still choosing. Big Screen does the same on the phones.
4. The first player to win the set number of rounds (3, 5 or 7) takes the match.

If a point drags past 45 seconds, overtime starts. Bullets begin to seek their target, and damage climbs every 10 seconds until someone falls.

## What's in it

- **Combat:** 130 health, 55 damage per shot (three plain hits kill), a 3-round magazine, and a 4-second block. Movement and bullets are tuned from frame-by-frame measurements of ROUNDS gameplay: low, snappy jumps (about four body-heights, plus a small double jump and wall jumps), and slower bullets that drop hard, so long shots are lobbed. Bullets fly as glowing tracers and throw sparks where they hit. An optional aim guide (off by default: turn it on in Settings) shows the first stretch of your shot's arc. Your own explosions hurt you a little, so don't fire explosive rounds point-blank. Your own shots do too if they come back at you (off a wall, falling back down, curling round): a quarter of the shot, at most 12% of your health, never the last of it, and only once the shot has bounced or been out 0.3 s, so spraying wildly has a price (`selfShot`).
- **56 cards** across gun, bullet, block and body upgrades, each with its own custom icon and animated art. Each card in a hand rolls 55% common, 32% uncommon, 13% rare, so about half of hands show a rare (and three drafts without one guarantee one). Cards stack, so combos multiply. Many change how you move or fight, not just your numbers: Limpet Mines stick to walls and blow half a second later (careful, they hurt you too), Bulwark raises a bullet-stopping wall when you block, Cluster Guard tosses bombs when you block, Frost Guard freezes a nearby rival, Aegis soaks a hit. **Fighting-game cards** (like a platform fighter): **Launcher** (rare) makes knockback grow with how hurt your rival is (up to x3, +50% per copy) and a launched rival who slams into a wall, ceiling or the arena edge **crashes** for 22 more; **Juggler** hits an airborne rival for 40% more and pops them back up; **Combo Chain** adds 15% per hit landed within 1.2 s of the last (up to +90%; getting hit breaks it, and the screen shows the hit count); **Uppercut Guard** makes your block pop every rival within 170 px straight up for 12. `smash.js` checks all four. (Launcher replaced Gravity Well.)
- **Legendary: Sun Lance** (the one Legendary card; gold card that breathes a glow, `r:3`). Two ways to fire (`LANCE_*`, `lanceStep`): **tap** for a quick, light beam (a 0.12 s wind-up, 40% of the full hit, a thinner ray), or **hold** to charge it: the longer the hold, the stronger and wider the beam, reaching the full hit at 0.75 s (a pulsing white ring and a bright ping say it's full); let go to fire, and a full charge held another 0.5 s lets go by itself. While winding up and charging: a dashed aim line to where the beam will stop, a spiked gold star swelling at the muzzle with embers drawn into it, a rising breath of fire. On touch screens and phone controllers, holding the aim stick charges (dragged: aimed; not dragged: at the nearest rival) and letting go fires; a quick tap fires the light beam; back in the stick's middle calls a charge off. Online, the guest's own screen runs its charge and the host fires the beam at the strength it reached (`q[10]`, `lw`). The beam is drawn like the card art: a white-hot core in a gold ray wrapped in ragged flame that licks back along it, with a heavy recoil kick. **The whole beam burns**, not just its head (`beams`, `beamStep`): anyone who walks into it or is crossed by it while it still shows (in flight, then 0.45 s as it fades) takes the hit, once per beam; a block soaks it and the shooter is never burnt by their own. x2.7 damage at full charge and it sets the rival on fire (another 25% of the hit over 2 s). It burns straight through wooden planks, leaving a charred hole through the plank for the rest of the round (embers that cool, last flames, smoke); stone and walls stop it. One beam a magazine and a reload 20% faster; fans, bursts and splits from other cards don't apply. Its own sound: a deep whoomph, a roar of flame and crackles (`shotLance`, `lanceHum`, built from `roar`). Odds (`legendRoll`): about 3% of hands once two rounds have been played, 6% for a player who's behind, one per player, never two in the same draft; Survival's shop sells it now and then from round 14 for 22 coins; never in boss loot or enemy decks. CPUs read the wind-up (block or jump out of the line if they guess they have time) and wait for a steady aim; they tap up close and charge part-way from range. Tuned with `scripts/balance.js` (holder wins about 44% of CPU points, the same as the old fixed wind-up); `tests/lance.js` checks tap vs charge strength, auto-release, the wind-up display, beam contact after it lands and mid-flight, one beam a magazine, wood vs stone, burn, overrides and odds; `tests/padlance.js` checks the phone controller's hold-to-charge.
- **Venom and fire on a fighter:** venom coats them in green slime with bubbles that rise and pop and drips that fall; fire scorches them darker with tongues of flame on top and embers. Ticks of either no longer flash the fighter white (only the hit does). Both come from the synced `ail` bits, so every screen shows them.
- **Comeback cards:** Afterburner (hits strap a rocket on your rival), Jammer Rounds (hits delay their block), Pickpocket (hits steal a bullet), Detonator (stick up to 5 charges, block to blow them) and Grudge (hit harder for every round you're behind). Each shows on the fighter: the booster and flame, static, blinking charges, an ember crown.
- **Explosions you feel:** painted fireballs and smoke, stone chunks and dust shaken off the arena, scorch marks that stay for the round, a zoom punch and a warm flash (toned down with reduced motion).
- **36 room arenas, the one library every mode plays on** (duels, Versus free-for-all and 2v2, Big Screen, Co-op and Survival; each is 1920 x 1080 with four spawns, so up to four fighters fit in any of them; the older open arenas and their Wide copies stay in the code only for the tests of their moving parts). The newest eight: **Aqueduct** (a broken stone channel overhead, a bucket swinging through the break), **Market** (stalls with stone counters and wooden awnings, a fountain pad, a sign hung on a rope, crates), **Beacon** (a lighthouse with a lift up its shaft to the lamp room), **Cinder Pit** (stone shores stepping down to embers, planks hung over them), **Undercroft** (a cellar under a stone ceiling with trapdoor gaps, a chandelier beam above), **Barricade** (a tall wall with a crawl gap, toppling block towers either side), **Escalators** (lifts riding diagonally up to a stone mesa) and **Gatehouse** (a gate tower with a drawbridge raised on each side of its top). ROUNDS-style: chunky platforms, cover blocks, symmetric layouts, every climb within one jump plus an easy double jump) with one-way planks, solid walls, pits, hanging blocks that swing when you shoot or land on them, moving platforms that carry you, and bounce pads. - **ROUNDS-style tricks** (from studying a ROUNDS tips guide):
  - **Duck** by holding `S` on the ground: your hitbox squashes flat and head-height shots sail over. In the air, `S` cuts a jump short or drops you fast.
  - **Grip walls** by holding toward them: you barely slide, and your air jump comes back. Jump while holding into the wall to climb it; jump without holding to kick off it.
  - **Perfect parry:** a block that meets a bullet in its first 0.12 s sends it back at the shooter (PARRY).
  - **Edge launches:** block as you hit the arena border to bounce off it unhurt: off the bottom you launch about halfway up the screen with your air jump back (enough to get onto a platform from any gap; `edge-save.js` checks every arena), off a side you rocket back in, off the top you spike down.
  - **Hanging blocks hurt:** shoot one so it swings hard into your rival to smash them; block to knock it back.
  - New cards: **Echo Guard** (every block fires a second free block a beat later) and **Shield Charge** (blocking dashes you along your aim and rams your rival). Long Haul bullets now grow as they fly, like ROUNDS' Grow.
- **Blink Guard teleports you can see:** the body collapses into a ring where it was, a streak and fading afterimages trace the jump, and it pops out with a flash and a rising zip. - **Card art**: every card and character has a painted illustration (`cards/*.webp`, about 20 KB each, made with Higgsfield in the game's own style). On the card it drifts slowly, a light sweeps across and sparks in the card's colour rise, faster when hovered; images load on demand and warm up in idle time, and a card whose image is missing falls back to its drawn animation.
- **Chat** (online duels and Co-op): the speech-bubble button next to Pause (or Enter on a keyboard) opens a message bar with quick messages (GG, Nice shot!, Rematch?, 😂, 🔥). Messages show for a few seconds at the bottom left (top left on phones), 120 characters at most, one every 0.7 s, always as plain text. Settings > Chat turns it off both ways. `browser-online.js` checks it between two real browsers.
- **Party · 2v2** (Multiplayer, online; and Single player > Party vs CPUs): two against two on five party arenas built for it (1920 x 1080, shown whole on any screen, phones too, two spawns per side, every level a jump apart and nothing stacked awkwardly under anything else), each with its own character: **Plaza** (wide open, long sightlines: long-range builds shine), **Islands** (shores over pits, islands, pads to high ledges: mobility and lobs), **Gearworks** (close quarters, a pit room with one lift: bouncing and blasting shots), **Ropeyard** (three rope drops, crates, a middle plank: patient, tactical play), **Spire** (hold the central tower: arcing and homing shots dig you out). In the lobby everyone, the host too, picks **Team Orange** (orange and light apricot fighters) or **Team Blue** (blue and light sky blue), so teammates read as one colour, and in a party name tags are tinted in the team's colour; two to a team, a friend who joins lands on the team with fewer players, and CPUs fill any empty seat at Start. Teammates' shots pass through each other. A team takes the point when the other is wiped out (the first side to fall loses: no trades); two points win the round. The **losing pair draft together**, like every versus draft: each player picks from their own five cards at the same time, and nobody sees anyone else's hand, only what they took when the cards flip. CPU players go by names (Bolt, Pip, Rook, Nova), and teammates are shades of one colour (orange and gold, blue and light blue). A friend who leaves mid-match sits out and the rest play on (in a rematch their seat is a CPU). Online, the host runs the match and keeps a link to each friend: their own input queue, their own snapshot clock (60 a second on a direct link, 20 through the relay) and their own effects queue; each screen predicts its own fighter and draws the other three from the host's snapshots. Tested with four screens on a simulated network: every screen draws the others within about 2.5 px of the host's path, with judder near 1.3% (40 ms and 90 ms + jitter).
- **On a game website:** CUUED runs inside another site's `<iframe>` (a Friv-style portal such as Flick). `node scripts/build.js --portal` packages it as `dist/portal/cuued/` (+ `.zip`): the game page, card art, icons, a 480x360 thumbnail, a 1200x630 banner and `game.json` (title, description, tags, orientation, iframe settings, events). Inside a frame, invite links and Big Screen QR codes point at the site's page (`?share=`), `?join=` joins a room, the site hears `ready` / `gameplayStart` / `gameplayStop` / `gameOver` and can send `pause` / `resume` / `mute` / `unmute` / `config`; arrow keys never scroll the site's page and the Add to Home Screen prompt is left to the site. The relay credentials endpoint answers extra sites listed in the `PORTAL_ORIGINS` env var. Full guide: `PORTAL.md`; `scripts/browser-portal.js` checks it all in real browsers with a stand-in site.
- **Spawns and smoothness (audited):** `spawns.js` places every fighter in every arena and mode (a duel, a 3- or 4-player free-for-all, 2v2, Survival with 1 to 4 heroes and its waves) and checks nobody starts in a wall, over a pit, on top of another fighter or falls out. Survival spots are chosen from fixed floor under the whole body (never something that swings, falls or breaks), sized for big fighters, enemies apart from each other and well away from every hero; extra heroes start beside the first. `versus.js` measures how smoothly every screen (the host's too) draws the other fighters. Every phone screen keeps clear of the notch / camera island on either side.
- **Multiplayer = Versus or Co-op Survival.** Each asks where everyone is: **Own screens** (everyone on their own phone or computer) or **One big screen** (this computer is the TV, phones are controllers); a smaller row joins any room with a code. Versus on own screens opens the same kind of lobby as a Big Screen: the code, a QR code and a Copy/Share invite link, the players (the host first, friends as they join, up to 4) and the rules: Mode (Free-for-all or Teams 2v2), rounds to win, card picks, CPU players, CPU skill, arena and health. Two players and no CPUs is a duel; a free-for-all of 2 to 4 is scored per fighter, everyone drafts on their own screen; Teams fill empty seats with CPUs; one player can start against a CPU. `versus.js` plays all four through the mock PeerJS.
- **Customize on a phone, and stacked hats:** held upright, Customize puts your fighter big at the top (drawn larger on its stage everywhere), what you've picked with its Buy / Wear button beside it, then the tabs and a grid that scrolls on its own. Hats stack: one per spot on the head (on top, ears or horns, a headband, a halo; up to four), tapping one you own puts it on or takes it off, a hat for a spot you've filled swaps in, and a halo floats higher over a tall hat. Everyone sees your stack online and on a Big Screen.
- **Invite links** are `cuued.com/?join=CODE` (old `#CODE` links still work, and a link opened in a tab where CUUED is already running joins too); on a phone, Share link opens the share sheet.
- **Survival, later rounds:** the swarm slot in the five-round wheel takes turns with **waves** (three waves of a few raiders each, the next storming in about a second after the last is down, +2 scrap per wave). From round 10 raiders can carry a **trait** (Armored, Swift, Venomous, Bouncy, Blinking, Vampiric: a name, an aura colour and a matching card), more often as the run goes on; enemy health climbs faster after round 12; and every beaten boss drops **boss loot**, one of three rare cards free for every hero before the shop. **Revives:** a downed co-op hero has a window (12 s, then 10, then 8 in the same round) for a teammate to stand in the circle for 2 s; if nobody does, they're out until the round ends. The circle is filled, ringed in their colour, with a clock running round it (red near the end) and the countdown above. **CPU packs** spread out: each keeps its own range, every other one flanks round the far side, a crowded one steps away, and one stuck against a moving piece tries the other way.
- **Co-op Survival on a Big Screen** (Multiplayer > Co-op Survival > One big screen, or Mode: Co-op Survival in the Big Screen lobby): the laptop or TV runs the run and 1 to 4 phones are the heroes, each its own colour. Every phone chooses its character and free card on its own screen while the big screen shows a column per hero (who is still choosing, their build); between rounds every phone gets its own shop (five full-size offers to swipe through: tap to buy, Lock, Reroll, tap a build card twice to sell it for half, then Ready), and the next round starts when every phone is ready. The phone's top bar shows the round, the team's lives, its scrap and level; a downed hero's phone says how long until they're back (or that a teammate is reviving them). Waves grow with the team (one more enemy per extra hero from round 4, tougher bosses). A friend who scans the code mid-run joins at the next round, as levelled and built as the hero with the least; a phone that leaves is out (nobody waits on it) and gets its hero back when it rejoins. When the lives run out every phone gets its CUUED coins and its own record (which unlocks characters), and the screen offers a New run or Back to lobby. `bigsurv.js` plays it through the mock PeerJS; `scripts/browser-bigsurv.js` with real browsers.
- **Big Screen** (a way to play Duel or Party: Multiplayer > Duel a friend or Party · 2v2 > **One big screen**; phones use Multiplayer > Join with a code, which joins any room): a laptop or TV is the screen everyone watches and has no fighter of its own; 1 to 4 friends join on their phones by scanning the QR code in the lobby (or typing the 4-letter code at cuued.com), and each phone becomes a controller. The phone draws no game: it shows your colour and name, your health bar, the score, Find me (rings and an arrow over your fighter on the screen, also shown at every point's start), the same twin-stick controls as phone play (left thumb moves and flicks up to jump; right thumb Brawl Stars style: drag to aim with the aim guide drawn on the big screen, let go to fire, a quick tap auto-fires at the nearest rival; with a wall in the way it fires the nearest angle or lob whose arc reaches them, leading a lob to where they'll be, and only with no way through does it aim straight at them), big Jump and Block panels down the right edge (Block fills as it cools down), vibration on hits, being knocked out and winning a point (Android), and keeps the screen awake. The lobby on the screen has the seats (with each phone's ping) and the game settings as tiles you step through either way (‹ ›, the arrow keys, or a click): **Free-for-all** (four colours: orange, blue, green, pink) or **Teams 2v2** (phones choose their team), rounds to win, who picks cards after a round (the losers, everyone, or nobody), **CPU players** (none by default; the host adds 1 to 3, and their skill), arena (all party arenas or one), health (75 / 100 / 150%) and round twists. Card drafts happen on the phones, everyone at once (25 s; a card is picked for anyone who doesn't), five full-size cards in a row you swipe through (dots show where you are; tap one to pick it): the screen shows a face-down card per picker that ticks as each locks in, then flips them face up one by one so the room sees every pick. A friend who joins mid-match takes a CPU's seat, or gets a new fighter from the next point (with as many cards as the player with the fewest); a phone that drops and comes back gets its own seat back. The screen can pause (the phones say so), rematch, or go back to the lobby to change settings with everyone still connected. Each phone sends its controls on the unordered fast channel on every touch and every frame (presses, shots and taps as running counters, so a lost packet loses nothing), and the host applies the newest one the moment it arrives, with no buffer; the phone gets a small status 15 times a second instead of game snapshots.
- **Big Screen room size:** every Big Screen room seats 4 phones, for everyone (the owner's call: no paid player limit). The seat-cap code (`bsCap`, `BS_FREE`/`BS_PLUS`, locked seats, the friendly "room is full" message) stays in place with both set to 4, so a limit could be set later.
- **Phone as controller** (Single player > Phone as controller): Big Screen for one. The game plays on this screen and your phone is the controller. The lobby (titled Phone controller, or Solo Survival) starts with a CPU opponent already seated (if CPU players was None, it goes back to None when you leave), and the match starts on its own a moment after the phone joins, so you never walk back to the computer to press Start. Mode can be Free-for-all against 1 to 3 CPUs or Survival. When a match or run ends, the phone shows Play again, which starts the rematch on the screen. `scripts/browser-solopad.js` checks it in real browsers.
- **Lava:** the ember floors (`kind 5`, on Cinder Pit, Greenhouse, Belfry and others) burn and throw you up, and are drawn live as lava (`drawLava`) so nobody mistakes them for ground: a glowing melt with brighter currents, cooled-rock rafts drifting across it with hot seams, bubbles that swell and pop, a rippling bright skin, heat haze above and sparks rising off it (timed, not from `Math.random`, so seeded tests don't shift). Reduced motion keeps the look and stills it; slow devices skip the sparks.
- **Phone controller reliability and aim** (Big Screen phones and a phone paired to your own computer): the phone sends its controls at most every 14 ms (30 ms over the relay; a typo once let it send on every touch event and every frame, which flooded slow links until the screen saw no fresh packet for 300 ms and stood the fighter still). A backed-up link drops a stale controls packet instead of queueing it. A stick whose finger left without a `touchend` (a system gesture, a notification) lets go on the next touch instead of ignoring that side forever, and both sticks let go when the phone goes to the background. Aim: a thumb resting in the aim stick's middle keeps the last aim (only letting go there cancels); with the thumb up the gun turns toward the nearest rival at a set speed rather than snapping; a silent phone leaves the aim where it was. A phone paired to your own computer sets the gun's angle directly (`cpadAim`), so a mouse pointer lying on the desk can't pull the aim around or flip it onto your fighter as the camera moves; moving the real mouse (`mouse.realAt`) hands the aim back to it. The phone also sends `fh` (thumb on the aim side) and `xc` (cancels) for hold-to-charge. Checked by `tests/padlance.js` and `tests/pairpad.js`.
- **Co-op Survival** (Multiplayer, online): the same run with a friend. The host's room is opened from Multiplayer > Co-op Survival; the friend joins with the code or link like a duel. The host runs the whole fight (every enemy, boss telegraphs, capsules, twists, strikes) and sends it to the guest; each hero has their own character, XP, levels, scrap and shop, and picks on their own screen at the same time (the next round starts when both are done). A downed hero comes back after 8, 12, then 16 s, or at once when the partner stands within reach of the spot for 2 s; both down at once costs a life. Raiders are 30% tougher and from round 4 there is one more of them; bosses are 75% tougher (they face two guns). Co-op fights on wider arenas: every standard arena 35% wider (except Springs, Piston and Chimes, built for their width) plus the big ones, and each boss's own arena widened, so two heroes and a boss have room; solo keeps the standard arenas, which are built for one-on-one. `coop.js` plays runs over the mock network (40 ms, and 90 ms with jitter).
- **Bosses** (in Survival, every 5th round): a boss has a signature deck, more health and size, and a special attack it always warns you about. **The Sniper** tracks you with a red laser for 0.85 s (still for the last 0.2 s), then fires a fast, heavy shot, and stands still while aiming; **The Brute** crouches and shakes with a warning lane for 0.75 s, then charges: jump it, block it at the last moment to stagger it, or bait it into a wall or the arena edge (a staggered boss takes 50% more damage). At 66% and 33% health it slams down new cards. **The Bombardier** (Bunkers) marks red circles on the ground for 1 s, then shells them. **The Phantom** (Archipelago) shimmers for 0.7 s, then blinks behind you. **The Warden** (Crossroads) raises a dome for 2.4 s that reflects your shots, then is left tired and open. The order of the five is shuffled each run; every 25th round is **the Crown** (Towers), which uses the snipe, then the charge, then the barrage as its health falls.
- **Sandbox:** hand yourself or the CPU any cards you like, as many copies as you want, and try them out with no drafting. Press Tab mid-fight to change cards; the CPU can fight back, dodge without shooting, or stand still as a target, and you can lock the arena.
- **Instant shots for the joining player:** their bullet leaves the gun the moment they click (a predicted bullet with the same speed, drop, walls and bounces), and the host fires the real one on the exact same physics step; when it arrives it takes over the predicted bullet's place and trail. Every bullet on the joining player's screen is placed on the host's clock and glides between updates.
- **Shots leave the gun on both screens:** the joining player draws the host's fighter a moment in the past, so the host's bullets are drawn on that same timeline when fired and ease onto the present over 0.2 s (so they still meet the joining player where it really is); snapshots carry each bullet's age so it stays hidden until the moment it was fired. Median distance from the host's gun when a shot first appears: 24 -> 20 px at 40 ms, 44 -> 10 px at 90 ms. The fighters themselves are always a network trip apart between the two screens (40-80 px while running), as in any online shooter; lag compensation makes shots count against what each player sees.
- **Smooth on any screen:** physics runs in fixed 1/120 s steps, and both screens draw every stepped thing (fighters, moving platforms) at the exact point between its last two steps, so 60, 120 and 144 Hz screens and uneven frame times never show 1-, 2- or 3-step jumps. Snapshots are stamped with the time their physics step represents (not the frame that sent them), and the joining player's playback clock eases toward the host's instead of jumping when a packet is early or late. A stalled frame (up to 0.25 s) is caught up rather than dropped, so neither side's simulation falls behind. `net.js` scores drawn movement on both screens under realistic timing (`NET_REAL`: 144 Hz screens with jitter; a 30 fps visitor with 150 ms stalls).
- **Switching tabs mid-match:** browsers pause a hidden tab's game, so each side tells the other ("…'s game is in the background"), and a visitor who switches away stands still on the host instead of repeating its last input.
- **Fair hits for the joining player (lag compensation):** the joining player sees the host's fighter a moment in the past, so each of its input steps says which moment it was showing, and the host checks its shots against where the host's fighter was then (up to 300 ms back). A shot that hits on the joining player's screen hits. In the bots' online test the joining player went from landing about half as many hits as the host to about the same.
- **60 updates a second:** on a direct or TURN link the host sends 60 snapshots a second (about 26 KB/s) instead of 30, and the joining player's smoothing buffer drops from 55 to 38 ms, so the host's fighter, bullets, health and hits show up sooner and move more smoothly. A relayed match stays at 20.
- **No lag spikes from lost packets:** PeerJS's data channel is fully reliable and ordered, so on real Wi-Fi one lost packet froze every later snapshot until it was resent (about a quarter second), then everything arrived at once. Snapshots, the visitor's inputs and pings now ride a second, lossy and unordered WebRTC channel (negotiated on the same connection, no extra signalling): a lost one is skipped and the next arrives 16-33 ms later. Picks, results and effects stay reliable. The visitor also draws the other fighter a little further in the past when snapshots arrive unevenly, so it always has a real position to glide to. `net.js` runs with 2% packet loss to keep it that way.
- **Smooth online play:** the joining player's game runs hanging blocks and moving platforms itself and steers toward the host's, and draws the other fighter a few milliseconds in the past along its true path, so nothing shakes between network updates. `net.js` checks both.
- **Online 1v1** by room code or invite link, peer-to-peer, with no accounts.
- **CPU opponent** at three skill levels. It reacts to where you *were* a moment ago, swings its aim at a human speed, crosses gaps, climbs walls and blocks.
- **Original music and sound**, generated live with the Web Audio API: a looping minor-key groove that ducks during card picks, with a surprise drum fill now and then (a snare roll, a hi-hat stutter or an off-beat snare groove at the end of a phrase, landing on a light splash: about one every 40 s, a little more often on a boss, a last life or match point), and effects with a room reverb so impacts boom. Music and volume settings; no audio files.
- **The sky tells the score:** each match starts at night and moves toward dawn as the leader nears the winning score. The last round is announced as FINAL ROUND.
- Reduced-motion support: screen shake and flashes are toned down when the OS asks for less motion.

## Project layout

```
src/counterpick.html   the game: one self-contained file (HTML, CSS, JS)
vercel.json            serves the repo as a static site on Vercel (cuued.com)
api/turn.js            Vercel function: optional Cloudflare TURN credentials for online play
scripts/relay-smoke.js live check of the public MQTT relays (not in CI)
scripts/turn-check.js  live check of the Cloudflare TURN relay: real authenticated allocation (not in CI)
scripts/balance.js     CPU-vs-CPU win rate of every card on its own (not in CI)
scripts/pacing.js      full CPU matches: point length, close matches, comebacks, overtime (not in CI)
scripts/browser-online.js  a real online match between two headless Chrome windows over PeerJS: bots play with every block and
                       movement card; checks errors, fps, snapshot rate and host/visitor agreement on both screens (not in CI)
scripts/browser-mobile.js  emulates a phone held sideways with real touch events: sticks, buttons, layout, rotate prompt (not in CI)
scripts/reach.js       drives a fighter over every arena (with a 12% jump-height margin) and fails if any platform, wall top,
                       hanging block or moving platform can't be reached from both spawns (not in CI: ~80 s)
scripts/stress.js      long CPU matches with big random card stacks: crashes, NaN, runaway bullets, stalls (not in CI)
scripts/browser-perf.js headless Chrome frame times at 1x and 2x pixel density, up to hundreds of bullets (not in CI)
scripts/browser-flow.js headless Chrome walkthrough: name, menu, draft, pause, settings, and a real online match on the live site (not in CI)
index.html             generated from src/ by scripts/build.js; adds the standalone page shell
scripts/build.js       node scripts/build.js [--check]
tests/                 headless test suite (plain Node, no dependencies)
```

Edit `src/counterpick.html`, then run `node scripts/build.js` to regenerate `index.html`. CI fails if you forget.

## Tests

```
node tests/run-all.js
```

The tests load the game headlessly with a stubbed DOM and canvas, then check:

- **`audit.js`** plays CPU vs CPU and fails if a bullet spawns past a wall, a bullet tunnels through one, a body ends a frame inside a wall, a point stalls, or the game throws.
- **`ceiling.js`** fires shots up out of every arena and fails unless each one flies out over the top edge and falls back in (the energy border stops fighters, not bullets).
- **`net.js`** loads two copies of the game, connects them through a mock PeerJS with 40 ms latency, and has bots play a full online match. It runs at 40 ms and on a laggy, jittery link (90 ms ± 45 ms, like a cross-country connection), each also in a `features` mode where both fighters carry every block/movement card and the bots duck, parry, dash and launch off edges while both screens draw, and fails if the guest's own fighter ever ends up inside or passes through a wall. (A Blink Guard teleport the two screens disagree on is settled by the host a few steps later; that quarter second isn't counted as a prediction jump.) It checks joining by code, drafting on both screens, input relay, view sync, rematch, a full room, a wrong code and disconnects. It also plays a relayed match through a mock MQTT broker (which parses the real packets) with the direct link blocked and the first broker down.
- **`movement.js`** runs the same input timeline at 30, 60, 120, 144 and 240 fps and checks that run speed, jump height, tap-jump height and long-jump distance match. Physics advances in fixed 1/120 s steps on every device.
- **`points.js`** checks scoring (the first death decides the point; there are no trades, so every point goes to Orange or Blue) and the arena edges (damage and knock-back, a small pop off the bottom, no clinging to edges).
- **`stacking.js`** fires every card stacked 1, 3, 6 and 9 times, plus 300 random mixed decks, and fails if a deck shoots nothing, its bullets don't fly, or anything goes NaN.
- **`cpu-reach.js`** checks that the CPU can get to you and land a hit on every arena, from either spawn.
- **`combos.js`** checks that you and the CPU can wall-jump up the arena's blocks but never climb its edges, that your own explosions hurt you a little, that Sandbox loadouts apply with no drafting, that stacked cards keep both effects (Limpet Mines with Splinter Rounds, double Ricochet), that the block cards work (Cluster Guard bombs, Frost Guard freeze, Double Guard's second charge), and that blocking into the bottom edge saves you and launches you high.
- **`card-art.js`** renders all 44 card animations and icons for 12 seconds each.
- **`turn.js`** checks the relay function: STUN fallback, Cloudflare credentials, port-53 filtering, CORS.
- **`bigscreen.js`** links a Big Screen host and up to four phone controllers through the mock PeerJS (40 ms, and 90 ms ± 45 ms): phones become controllers, the lobby seats them, an at-once draft, a press reaching the screen within a few frames, Find me, a whole free-for-all, a phone leaving and getting its own seat back, a late friend taking the CPU's seat (or a new fighter when there are no CPUs), the result on every phone, back to the lobby, and Teams 2v2.
- **`plusrooms.js`** checks Big Screen room size: 2 phones on a free screen (off Flick and on), a friendly full message for the 3rd, one Plus offer per room, 4 phones with Plus on the screen (none of them members, all told "Hosted with Flick Plus"), Plus switching off in the lobby and mid-match dropping nobody, a returning phone getting its seat back, and Co-op Survival following the same rule.
- **`drill.js`** checks that Ghost Rounds drill like Drill Ammo (through a thin wall or a thick block, stopped by very thick rock and by a block, deeper with two copies, at a reload cost) and that Survival opponents pick up power-ups.
- **`rigs.js`** drives every rig arena: each support breaks and its piece reacts (beam swings then falls, bridges come down and meet then drop away, the ball swings then drops, lifts trade height under a fighter then fall, a keystone brings its stack down sideways), a falling beam hurts, a fighter walks across the lowered bridges, nothing ends up inside a block (also through a minute of CPU play on each), and a guest fed the host's snapshots 90 ms late shows every piece where the host has it.
- **`audio.js`** plays every sound effect into a mock Web Audio graph and checks its parameters.

GitHub Actions runs the same suite on every push (`.github/workflows/test.yml`). The tests need Node 20 or newer.
